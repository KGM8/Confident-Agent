					Learning outcomes:
				
				
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Learn by writing and drawing about Mapungubwe
				
			
			
				
					EXAMPLE ACTIVITY: TRAVELERS DIARY
				
				
					Research project
				
			
			
				
					&nbsp;
					This activity is a great and fun way for learners to remember more about what they learnt on Mapungubwe. For this activity students will take on the role of a traveler like Marco Polo. They can be taking the role of a trader, a general traveler or someone visiting a relative who lives in Mapungubwe.
					&nbsp;
					The will write a dairy of their stay in Mapungubwe. They should also draw what they see during their stay in Mapungubwe.
					&nbsp;
					[Ensure the students have a bibliography attached at the end of their diaries. Using Harvard method.]
					&nbsp;
					The students should write about at least 3 of the following areas:
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The geography of the city of Mapungubwe. How does the city look like etc.
					2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Trade practices – describe what was traded, with whom, using the river/ports used.
					3.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Describe the society – rich and poor
					4.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The history of the earliest settlers of Mapungubwe. For example: I was told by my brother that the very first citizens of Mapungubwe were here from 1000ad to 1300ad and were Stone Age settlers.
					5.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Attending a burial in the city. Both a rich and poor burial – artifacts that will be buried with the person and location of the burial.
					6.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Mapungubwe state – economy, king and queen. It was a global superpower because of trade and one of the state’s leading the first stages of globalization.
					7.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Importance of gold in Mapungubwe
					8.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Farming practices
					&nbsp;
					
						
							
								
									&nbsp;
								
							
						
					
					&nbsp;
					Give points for creativity – how descriptive they are and the amount of research they put into their diary.

Activity B

	
		
			
				
					ACTIVITY PLANNING OVERVIEW
				
				
					&nbsp;
				
			
			
				
					Subjects covered:
				
				
					English, History (social sciences),
				
			
			
				
					Connecting Classroom themes:
				
				
					&nbsp;
				
			
			
				
					Core skills:
				
				
					Analytical and critical thinking, communication, historical enquiry, creativity, writing skills
				
			
			
				
					Learning outcomes:
				
				
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Learn through group activity about trade
				
			
			
				
					EXAMPLE ACTIVITY: Traders
				
				
					Research project/group project
				
			
			
				
					&nbsp;
					This is a group activity. Divide the students into 4 groups – Mapungubwe trader, Chinese traders, Indian traders and Arabian traders.
					&nbsp;
					Have the students do a group research project on trade. Have them cover the following topics:
					&nbsp;
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The bartering system (the beginning of trade before money was introduced)
					2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Trade – why did people trade?
					3.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Trade and the beginning of globalization (How it connected cities, formed trade routes, Silk road etc.)
					[Report must have a bibliography at the back. Harvard style referencing]
					&nbsp;
					Role play as traders:
					Give them a list of the items their country trade and what items their countries need from the others. (including the quantity they have and need of the items)
					&nbsp;
					Have them colour/draw/write the items on strips of paper.
					Each group should have a section of the class to themselves which if they want they can decorate according to theme with pictures etc. If possible they can dress up.
					&nbsp;
					Give the students between 15 -20 minutes to roleplay as traders. See which group comes out on top and who doesn’t. At the end of the roleplay discuss how it was like to trade- was it easy of difficult etc.

					EXAMPLE ACTIVITY: Civilizations and water : Rivers the lifeblood of a civilization
				
				
					Research project
				
			
			
				
					&nbsp;
					Set a research project for the students on why the relationship between rivers/fresh water sources and civilizations were important. They should present this on a poster board and present it to the class.
					&nbsp;
					The civilizations the students can look at is: (if you want to make the presentation more varied)
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Mapungubwe
					2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Egypt
					3.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Mesopotamia
					4.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Any other civilization
					&nbsp;
					It is important students cover why their location to seas/rivers/fresh water sources was/is important to these civilization for:
					1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Farming
					2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Trade
					&nbsp;
					The report should cover the first settlers and also the death/decline of the civilization due to climate change etc.
